How the peg pyramid behaves

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs. Each row is wider than the one above it, so a ball has to make a series of left-or-right decisions on the way down. Over time, those tiny turns create a pattern that is easy to predict in the aggregate but impossible to steer in a single round.

Mathematically, the layout is very close to a Galton board. The middle pockets get hit more often, while the outer pockets are rarer and usually carry larger values. That is why the payout table looks balanced even though the outcomes on any one drop can feel uneven.
